    Vistfang afritað

    JOÐ, one evening, three doors

    Same evening, same sunset, same three pixel faces and the same terracotta. What changes is the shape of the page: the 3D world becomes the page, and everything else hangs off it or folds into one band. Nine sections become four.

    Numbered pins

    1. 1Sunset, kept. The hero stays as it is. The navigation shrinks to three doors, each a lantern: Heimurinn, Hópurinn, Hillan. The eight-lantern rail, the nine-item menu and the second lantern strip all go; the address stays the one action, in the hero and in the bar.
    2. 2The world is the page. BlueMap fills the viewport directly under the mesas, so the badlands sit on top of the real world. It opens as a still: a screenshot taken at sync time. The three.js viewer only boots when someone presses the lantern (or, on desktop with a good connection, when the frame scrolls into view). A visitor who never touches it never downloads it.
    3. 3Places rail. The eleven places, each with its photo, in one sideways rail on the map. Tap one and its postcard opens over the world. This replaces the Staðir grid and does the album's job. No "fly there": the 3D map covers the home area only, and the places are spread across the whole world.
    4. 4Drawn map is a toggle, not a section. "Teiknað kort" lays the painted paper map over the same frame, flags and all. Same admin editor, same data. "Myndir · 11" opens the whole wall as an overlay, in the admin's order. "Heill skjár" hands over to BlueMap's own page.
    5. 5Photos live in the world. No separate album section. Every picture is one tap away from the place it shows, and all of them are behind one button. Nothing is removed from the admin panel.
    6. 6Hópurinn, one band. The eight heads with a lantern for who is in, then the wanted board as five posters, one per category, with the top three on each: the leader large, second and third as two lines under the rule. All five categories are visible at once, so no tabs; the full ledger already exists on /crew and each player's page.
    7. 7Hillan. The packs on one slim strip: every pack a crate with its glyph, its label read out on the counter when pointed at, as the closed store does today. The store sign, the open-shelves state and the join steps go; the address already sits in the hero and the bar. If a third door feels like one too many, this strip can live without one.
    8. 8Campfire. Links lose Pakkaritill (removed entirely) and Heimskort (it is the page now). The duel becomes an easter egg: tap the fire.
    9. 9Phone. The three lanterns sit in a bottom bar, in reach of a thumb. The map is a 4:5 poster that opens the viewer full screen, so BlueMap's drag and pinch never fight the page scroll. The places rail, the posters and the crates scroll sideways instead of stacking.

    Performance

    • BlueMap behind a poster: no iframe, no WebGL, no 6 MB viewer until intent. On phones, never inline.
    • Poster made by the sync script (one screenshot at the start view), served at three widths.
    • Drawn map SVG mounts only when toggled; the album grid only when opened.
    • Two fixed bars instead of rail + bar + strip: fewer scroll listeners, one scroll-spy for three ids.
    • Dropping rp-editor removes three.js and jszip from the bundle and 30-odd files from the build.
    • Everything else already in place stays: self-hosted fonts, srcset photos, CSS scroll timeline for the sky.

    Open questions

    • Whether the duel stays at all. It costs nothing hidden behind the fire.

    In this prototype

    Player heads are drawn placeholders (the real ones come from mc-heads.net). Stats, online state and the sync date are examples. The map is a still and "Ferðast um heiminn" only mimics the load.
