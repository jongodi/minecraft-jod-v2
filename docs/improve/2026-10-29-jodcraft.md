# JOÐcraft: improve and design pass, 29 October 2026

Branch `improve/jodcraft-design`, seven commits on top of `ab584ef`. Nothing pushed or deployed.

## World bible

**World.** A Minecraft badlands at sunset: terracotta mesas in five clay bands, a town below in a valley, seen from the air. The visitor stands at the edge of this world, on the last ridge before it, and the page is one evening: the sun goes down with the scroll, the town's lanterns come on, and at the foot of the night there is a campfire.

**Materials.** Terracotta strata (red, orange, yellow, white, brown), oak planks and posts, paper slips nailed to wood, iron chains, lantern glass. Everything is cut on a two-pixel grid with notched corners and a bevel; nothing has a soft edge, a radius or a blur. Depth is a hard shadow straight down in the night's colour.

**Light.** One light source: the sunset on the horizon, then the lanterns. Amber lantern light is only on what can be pressed. The sky's four stops are the palette's origin; every other colour is a clay, a wood, a paper or the night.

**Palette origin.** The sky gradient (plum to gold), the badlands' clays, and the game's own chat colours (white, yellow, green, red with their quarter-strength shadows). Night is a warm off-black, never grey or blue-black, except the very top of the sky.

**Type voice.** Three pixel-drawn faces: Alfa Slab One for titles (the sign painter's slab), Pixelify Sans for every paragraph, Silkscreen for everything short and everything the server says (doors, buttons, counts, the address). Icelandic as an Icelander writes it; the game's own Icelandic for the game's own sentences.

**Motion character.** The game's: stepped, pixel-snapped, in whole steps (`steps()` on the clouds, the ridges, the climbers, the fire's frames). Smooth easing only for what the player drags or presses. Scroll is time: the sky, the ridges and the flight are driven by the scroll itself, on the compositor where the browser can. Under reduced motion the evening is held at one hour, the flight is a still, and every loop stops.

**Anti-world, three things it must never look like.** A corporate SaaS landing page (gradient blobs, three feature cards, glassy header); an esports or crypto site (neon, glow with no lamp, purple); a glossy 3D render or a Minecraft marketing page with smooth rounded cards.

**Direction chosen.** Keep the evening and sharpen it: fewer doors, the map the one focal point after the sunset, the system written down as tokens, and the first seconds on a phone kept for the hero.

## What changed

### Fixes
- The splash over the Ð never showed on a phone: under `(pointer: coarse)` the tap-target rule made the button `position: relative`, which pulled it into its zero-height wrapper's line box one em of the name's size down, below the hero's clip. Found in the verification walk, fixed, and verified by touch at 320, 360 and 390 px (pre-existing).
- The selects' chevron: the fields' own `background` shorthand reset the chevron's repeat and size, which tiled it across the field, and their padding covered it; the fields set the colour alone and keep room for it. The admin panel's selects stand on the night, so there it is drawn in paper. Verified signed in on the wall's place picker and the panel.
- The rail read its scroll width synchronously right after hydration and forced a layout of the whole page; it measures in the next frame now.
- The locator bar's tips (a mark's distance in blocks) were rewritten on every frame of the flight, which laid each mark out and painted it again for a word only a pointer can see; they are rewritten twice a second, or at once under a pointer.
- The F3 lines were rewritten on every frame of the flight (a text node laid out and painted each time); they are rewritten eight times a second.

### Performance
- The rooms' code and stylesheets (board, crew, shelf) were fetched at the browser's first idle moment, which on a slow phone was the hero's first seconds, and each stylesheet arriving restyled the whole page. They warm at the visitor's first scroll, touch or key, or five seconds after load.
- The flight's first two stills (the overview and the landing, about 180 kB) were fetched beside the hero's fonts and stylesheet; they wait for the load event unless the visitor is already scrolling.
- The hero's critters and lantern glow, and the fire's frames and smoke, pause while their section is off screen (`useAway`): an animation off screen still invalidated style on every frame of a scroll elsewhere.
- Every flickering lantern glow is its own layer, so the flicker runs on the compositor.
- The locator bar, the experience bar's fill, and the town's dusk and lights are composited, so moving or fading them repaints nothing under them.

### Design system
- Every colour role is now an `oklch()` value, converted so each returns the same 8-bit colour; the sky's four stops stay in sRGB on purpose, since the gradient between them was tuned by eye and interpolates in sRGB only while every stop is written in it.
- Fifty-nine `rgba(21, 16, 13, …)` literals and the brown, paper, ink, glint and pitch alphas are written from their tokens with relative colour syntax (`rgb(from var(--night) r g b / 0.35)`).
- New tokens: `--pitch` (the deepest dark), `--glint` (the light on a bevel), the game's chat palette with its quarter-strength shadows, an elevation scale of three hard drops (`--lift-1..3`, fainter the higher it hangs), `--dur-0` (a tap's answer, 60 ms), `--dur-4`, `--dur-step` (one pixel step), `--stagger`, and `--ease-step` (`steps(4, end)`).
- No stylesheet carries a bare millisecond any more; the fire, the kindling, the climbers, the splash pop, the town's lights, the posters' stagger and the wall's pop all read the tokens.
- Live counts (who is in, the countdown to a fire) are set in tabular numerals.

### Layout
- The bar: with three doors the whole address fits from 1100 px (it was 1200 px); the breakpoint rules and their comments say so.
- The hero's plate under the server's lantern carries one more line (the version and the shelf), so the shelf's way in is on the first screen, where the server is described. Nothing else in the hero moved; on a phone the map is still the one thing after the hero.

### Navigation
- Three doors: Heimurinn, Taflan, Eftirlýst. The shelf keeps its room and `#hillan` but has no door; it is reached from under the server's lantern in the hero, beside the version its packs belong to, with the count of what stands on it (counted on the server, with the seed list as fallback). The hotbar's three slots are a third wider for a thumb.
- The keys are 1 to 3; the splash says so; `aria-keyshortcuts` follows.
- The README and DESIGN.md say three doors.

### Motion
- The motion tokens above; the game's stepped character is now named (`--ease-step`, `--dur-step`) rather than written as numbers.
- Loops pause off screen (above). Under reduced motion nothing new moves: the shelf's line and the hotbar have no animation to lose.

### Immersion
- The page's own scrollbar is the plank's wood on the night (`scrollbar-color` on `html`), not a white track down a dark page.
- A `select` has no native arrow; a chevron drawn on the pixel grid in the ink stands in its place (the night's form and the walls' pin-to-place selects).
- The one-off drop shadows under tags, slips, signs and the strata under the bar are the three lifts of the token sheet.

## Budgets: met and not met

- Lighthouse 95 in all four: not met for performance (85 mobile; the other three are 100, 96 and 100, the 96 being the one console error this sandbox causes). The lab box is a four-core sandbox and the score moved between 82 and 93 across clean runs of the same build, so the gain over 81 is real but small: TBT fell from 500 to 380 ms, TTI from 5.0 to 4.5 s.
- LCP under 2.5 s: not met in the lab (3.1 s simulated; 2.3 s on the best run). In the lab the LCP element is the wordmark (text); its simulated render delay is the hero's stylesheet and the first script evaluation on a slow connection. The observed LCP on an unthrottled run is 250 ms.
- CLS under 0.1: met (0). INP under 200 ms: not measured in the lab; every tap answers in CSS within `--dur-0` (60 ms) and nothing synchronous runs on a tap.
- Map code not in the initial bundle: met. The 3D viewer is an iframe that only loads when the lantern is pressed or the flight wakes it; the rooms are dynamic imports.
- Scrolling at 60 fps at 4x CPU: not met during the flight. The flight is two full-frame canvases warped per frame over the sky's own layers; the per-frame cost that remains is the compositor commit of about eighty layers, which no CSS change cuts. Holding 60 fps at 4x would mean dropping the flight on phones, which is a call for Jón Goði (below). Outside the flight the page scrolls on the compositor.
- Canvas and WebGL DPR cap at 2: met (particles, the flight's sheets, and the viewer's own cap in `jod.js`).
- WCAG AA contrast, 44 px targets, keyboard: met and verified; every control was already checked against its surface in `tokens.css`. Skip link, focus rings, key numerals on the doors.

## Assumptions

- The shelf is the door to drop: the world is the page, the board is what a member reaches for first, the crew's room is the server's people; the shelf is what the server runs and belongs beside the version in the server's lantern.
- When the shelf is open no lantern in the bar is lit (it has no door); the room's own plank says where the visitor is.
- The sky's stops stay in sRGB; every other colour is OKLCH. Any gradient mixing a sky token and another now interpolates in OKLab (the flight's night sky, the board's night sky); dark to dark, the difference is not visible.
- Relative colour syntax (`rgb(from …)`) and `oklch()` need a 2023 browser (Chrome 119, Safari 16.4, Firefox 128). The crew's phones are newer than that.
- In this sandbox Redis and the map's blob store are unreachable, so `/api/playnight` answers 500 and the live map cannot load; both are the environment, not the site. The one console error in the Lighthouse runs is that 500, which costs best practices one audit here and none in production.
- The type ramp (14, 17, 20, then fluid) was kept: the two small steps are 1.21 and 1.18 rather than 1.25 or more, because the faces are pixel-drawn and the sizes were chosen for how their pixels land; changing them would ripple through every surface for no visible gain.

## Needs Jón Goði's call (skipped, listed)

- Whether the flight should be flown on phones at all, or only on devices with room (it already skips devices with little memory or few cores, and reduced motion). Dropping it on phones is the only way to the 60 fps budget there.
- Whether the shelf should also get a line in the crew room's foot (beside "Taflan" and "Veggir hópsins"), a second way in. Not done: one way in, on the first screen, is enough and keeps the room's foot to two.
- Major-version dependency upgrades: ESLint 8 (unsupported upstream) to 9, and `eslint-config-next` 15 to 16 to match Next 16. Not touched.
- Removing any user-visible content: nothing was removed.

## Not done, with reasons

- The 60 fps scroll budget during the flight, above.
- `/api/playnight` returning an error status while Redis is down: left as an error; the hero keeps its last answer and the room says so.
- A `tall` poster for phones (the landing still is fetched in landscape at 1920 px and the flight's own portrait landing beside it, about 250 kB together): a pipeline change to `map:poster`, out of scope for this pass.

## Feature ideas noticed, not built

- A "stack count" on the shelf's line: the pack count written as the game writes a stack's count on a slot, rather than in words.
- The locator bar's tips on a phone: a tap on a mark could say the distance in the chat, since the tooltip never shows there.
- A `tall` still of the town for phones, taken by `map:poster` with the flight's portrait frame.
- The hotbar's slots could carry the count of what is new on the board as the game writes a stack's count (the door already does on wide screens; the slot does too, but small).
